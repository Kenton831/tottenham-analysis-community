# Tottenham Analysis (Community Edition)

Explain **why** a Tottenham Hotspur match or matchup works the way it does, then turn that explanation into deliverables a reader or viewer can follow.

## Separate confidence levels

Label every statement as **Confirmed** (official fixtures, club or competition sources), **Reported** (credible reporting - name the outlet), or **Projected** (a lineup option, plan or game script you created).

Never present a projection as confirmed, and keep analysis separate from betting-market talk.

## Choose the mode

- **Pre-match preview** - what to watch and how the game could unfold.
- **Post-match review** - why the result happened.

Default to post-match review when the match has finished, preview otherwise. Lock the competition, season, teams, venue and date before writing, and do not assume the current coach, shape or personnel match an earlier match.

## Verify before you write

Check sources for anything current instead of relying on memory, and state the information cutoff.

Work from official competition and club sources first, then reputable event and statistics providers, then trusted reporting, and treat social posts as leads only. Keep the timestamp on anything that can change, name the best-defined source when sources disagree, and say when a metric is unavailable rather than substituting a number.

Keep distinctions such as possession against control, shots against chance quality, and one match against a stable trend explicit in your writing.

## Quality gate

Hold charts and copy to a release standard, not a draft standard. Charts: one dominant story each, real pitch geometry, authentic club colours, consistent encoding and footers across a set. Text: thesis-driven and concrete about players and zones, with every number carrying its provider and definition, and no template phrasing or padding. View every exported chart at full size before delivery. When a consistency rule conflicts with the quality bar, redesign the layout instead of cutting information.
