# Analysis workflow

An argument that only restates the score or the form table is not analysis. In both modes the reader should finish knowing what happened, why it happened, and which parts are repeatable.

## Pre-match preview

1. **Lock the fixture and the cutoff.** Competition, round, season, date, venue, how the tie can end, and the exact time information was last checked.
2. **Build the current baseline.** Recent matches rather than the season average: results, goals, shot volume and quality, set pieces, and the home and away split. Name the competition each sample comes from.
3. **Check availability before naming anyone.** Official sources first, each player marked confirmed, doubtful or out, and no projected starter promoted to confirmed.
4. **Profile the current Tottenham.** Current coach, shape, build-up, pressing trigger, progression routes, set-piece takers.
5. **Profile the opponent.** The same profile plus the two or three habits that decide matches against them.
6. **Build the tactical matchup.** Pair specific zones and players; two or three real matchups beat a list of ten.
7. **Define game scripts.** Conditional scenarios, labelled as scenarios rather than predictions.
8. **Present lineup options.** One to three projected options, each with its reasoning and the trade-off it accepts.
9. **Matchday watchlist.** Three to five concrete things to watch in the first twenty minutes.
10. **Quality gate.** Every number sourced, every projection labelled, no claim that cannot be checked after kick-off.

## Post-match review

1. **Lock the match record.** Competition, round, venue, date, final and half-time score, goalscorers with minutes, cards, substitutions, referee, and the source for the record itself.
2. **Write the thesis first.** One paragraph on why the match finished as it did; everything afterwards supports it or corrects it.
3. **Reconstruct in phases.** Split by behaviour rather than fixed fifteen-minute blocks, and say what changed between them.
4. **Explain mechanisms.** Who vacated which zone, which pass broke the line, which rotation freed a man. Claims must be checkable against an event or a clip.
5. **Check identities.** Verify names, numbers, roles and sides; do not attribute a phase to a player who was not on the pitch.
6. **Review players by role.** Judge them against the job they were given, and connect at least one interaction with a teammate.
7. **Audit the numbers.** Provider and definition for every metric; reconcile shot-level values with the published team total and report the gap instead of averaging.
8. **Limit turning points.** Two to four, each with minute, score state and effect.
9. **Evaluate coaching adaptation.** Separate the change, the intent and the effect; a substitution that failed is not automatically a mistake.
10. **Separate signal from noise.** Say what this match shows about the team and what needs more matches before it is a trend.
11. **State what the match does not prove.** Sample size, absent players, opponent context, open questions.
